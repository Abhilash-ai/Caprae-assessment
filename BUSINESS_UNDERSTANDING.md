### 1. What is Caprae's Mission?

Caprae Capital operates on a fundamental thesis that diverges sharply from the legacy private equity paradigm: lasting enterprise value is created through hands-on, post-acquisition operational transformation rather than spreadsheet-driven financial engineering. Where traditional PE firms prioritize debt leverage, cost extraction, and short holding horizons, Caprae approaches acquisition as the beginning of a seven-year journey. The mission is to partner with great founders, acquire high-potential small and mid-market businesses, and systematically elevate them into enduring, market-leading modern enterprises through operational rigor, technology adoption, and corporate governance.

At the core of this mission is the deployment of practical, production-grade Artificial Intelligence and modern software frameworks across the acquired portfolio. Caprae’s dual-engine model—combining Software as a Service (SaaS) and M&A as a Service (MaaS)—is architected to solve the primary failure mode of traditional M&A: the execution gap that occurs once the deal closes. By embedding tech-enabled operational playbooks, Caprae automates repetitive workflows, sharpens customer acquisition and retention, and enables data-driven strategic decision-making across legacy sectors that have historically lagged in digital adoption.

Ultimately, Caprae’s mission is about compounding human horsepower. It is built for operators who subscribe to the `#BleedandBuild` mindset—builders who treat company building with craftsmanship, urgency, and radical ownership. By institutionalizing world-class corporate governance infused with high-speed execution and cultural resilience, Caprae aims to demonstrate that private equity can be a catalytic engine for genuine growth, job creation, and long-term societal value rather than mere asset stripping.

### 3. How is Caprae Changing the ETA Space and Broader PE?

The Entrepreneurship Through Acquisition (ETA) ecosystem and traditional search fund models have historically faced critical structural bottlenecks. Traditional searchers spend 18 to 24 months grinding through manual deal sourcing, fragmented broker networks, and cold outreach spreadsheets before acquiring a company—only to face an even steeper cliff post-acquisition. Many search fund CEOs struggle with operational isolation, lacking the institutional infrastructure, technological tooling, and executive governance needed to scale a business beyond the founder's initial footprint. Search fund CEO termination issues and post-acquisition stagnation often stem from this lack of operational enablement.

Caprae is disrupting the ETA space by institutionalizing and democratizing the entire acquisition lifecycle through its MaaS (M&A as a Service) and tech-enabled post-acquisition model. Instead of leaving operators to reinvent the wheel, Caprae surrounds acquired businesses with a repeatable operational stack, AI-augmented workflow automation, and institutional-grade corporate governance. By treating M&A as a seven-year post-acquisition journey, Caprae shifts the focus from transaction closing to long-term compounding. This de-risks the transition for both the searcher and the acquired team, ensuring that operational momentum is maintained and accelerated from Day 1.

Across broader Private Equity, Caprae is providing a blueprint for how technology—specifically practical, agentic AI and modern full-stack workflows—can replace outdated financial engineering. Most mid-market PE firms rely heavily on multiple expansion and EBITDA debt slicing. Caprae proves that the greatest alpha comes from operational modernization: giving portfolio companies unfair advantages in lead generation, sales conversion, talent retention, and customer experience. By combining high-horsepower human talent with autonomous, intelligent tooling, Caprae is redefining what an agile, 21st-century investment and operating firm looks like.
